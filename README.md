# 🚗🦌 California Wildlife-Vehicle Collision Tracker

A comprehensive interactive website tracking animal crossings, accidents, and crashes across California with precise locations, annual statistics, and data from credible sources.

## 🌐 Live Website
**Visit the tracker:** https://tanishkatharanipathy-svg.github.io/california-wildlife-collision-tracker/

## 📊 Features

- **Interactive Map** - Visual hotspot locations with risk levels (Critical, High, Moderate)
- **Danger Rankings** - All 25 hotspots ranked by collision frequency
- **Real-time Data** - 48,000+ animals killed annually on California roads
- **Annual Statistics** - Year-by-year collision trends (2018-2023)
- **Species Breakdown** - Detailed data on mule deer, newts, bears, mountain lions, and more
- **Searchable Database** - Filter by location, region, species, and risk level
- **Statistical Charts** - Interactive visualizations of trends and species involvement
- **Mobile Responsive** - Works on desktop, tablet, and mobile devices

## 🎯 Hotspot Coverage

### Tier 1: DEADLIEST (Critical Risk)
- **Interstate 280** (Santa Clara/San Mateo) - $178,439/mile/year
- **Alma Bridge Road** (Santa Clara) - 5,000+ Pacific newts/year
- **Highway 17** (Santa Clara/Santa Cruz) - 200+ incidents/year

### Tier 2-5: HIGH to MODERATE Risk
- Sierra Nevada highways (4, 88, 108, US 50, 49)
- Northern California (I-5, HWY 20, 44, US 97, 299)
- Southern California (I-405, HWY 2, I-15, 67, I-8)
- Central Coast (US 101, HWY 1, 154)

## 🔍 Data Methodology

**Collection:**
- California Highway Patrol (CHP) incident reports
- California Roadkill Observation System (CROS) citizen science observations
- Caltrans official records

**Accuracy:**
- CROS data has 13-meter spatial accuracy
- >97% species identification rate
- Data spans 2009-2024 with continuous updates
- ~15,000 miles of California state highways monitored

**Limitations:**
- Actual collision numbers likely higher due to unreported incidents
- Underreporting common for incidents without human injury or major property damage

## 📖 Understanding the Data

### Risk Levels
- **CRITICAL:** >200 incidents/year or unique hazards
- **HIGH:** 80-200 incidents/year
- **MODERATE:** 30-80 incidents/year

### Species Priority
1. **Mule Deer** (48,000+) - Most common
2. **Pacific Newts** (22,000+) - Amphibian hotspot
3. **Coyotes** (2,000+) - Urban areas
4. **Other Mammals/Birds** (5,000+)
5. **Black Bears** (557) - High impact
6. **Mountain Lions** (300+) - Conservation concern
